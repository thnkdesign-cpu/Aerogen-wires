Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS) in Preterm Infants

[Image placeholder]
Condition Overview

What is RDS?

Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS) is a serious complication that can affect preterm infants. RDS occurs when a baby's lungs have not fully developed and are unable to produce sufficient amounts of surfactant, a naturally occurring substance that reduces surface tension and helps keep the tiny air sacs (called alveoli) in the lungs open during breathing.1,2

Without enough surfactant, the lungs become stiff and unstable, causing the air sacs to collapse after each breath. As a result, infants with RDS must work much harder to breathe and may struggle to obtain the oxygen their bodies need.1,2

RDS affects infants born prematurely; the earlier a baby is born, the greater the risk of developing the condition. While advances in neonatal care have significantly improved outcomes, RDS remains a major cause of illness and death among preterm infants worldwide.1,2

Symptoms & Signs

Signs and Symptoms of RDS

Symptoms of RDS typically appear shortly after birth and may include:1

  • Rapid breathing
  • Grunting sounds during breathing
  • Flaring of the nostrils
  • Chest retractions (pulling in of the chest wall during breathing)
  • Low blood oxygen levels
  • Typical chest X-ray appearance
Treatment Approaches

How RDS Is Treated

Treatment depends on the severity of the condition and may include:1,3

  • Supplemental oxygen
  • Respiratory support, such as nasal Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (nCPAP)
  • Surfactant replacement therapy for infants with more severe RDS

Early diagnosis and timely treatment are important to help improve breathing, reduce complications, and support healthy development.1,3

If your child has been diagnosed with RDS, your healthcare team can provide information about available treatment options and ongoing clinical research.4-7

Innovation & Research

Aerogen Pharma's Investigational Approach

Aerogen Pharma is developing APC-0101, an investigational drug-device combination product designed to deliver aerosolized surfactant to preterm infants with RDS receiving non-invasive respiratory support.4-7

This investigational therapy is being evaluated in clinical studies and has not been approved for commercial use. It is available only through authorized clinical trials conducted under applicable regulatory requirements.7
