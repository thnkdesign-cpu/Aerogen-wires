Current Treatment and Therapy Approaches

The treatment for RDS is focused on supporting breathing, improving oxygen delivery, and addressing the underlying surfactant deficiency that contributes to lung instability. Current treatment approaches include:1

  • Non-invasive respiratory support
  • Oxygen therapy
  • Surfactant replacement therapy
  • Other supportive neonatal care

Innovation & Access

The Need for Continued Innovation

Despite significant advances in neonatal medicine, important challenges remain in the treatment of RDS.1-3

Many current therapies require specialized equipment, invasive procedures, or highly trained clinical staff. These barriers can limit access to life-saving care, particularly in resource-constrained healthcare settings.2,4

Researchers and innovators continue to explore new approaches that may simplify treatment, reduce invasiveness, expand access to care, and improve outcomes for vulnerable preterm infants around the world.3-6

Advancing respiratory therapies that can be delivered more efficiently and less invasively remains an important area of focus in neonatal medicine.3-6

Future Delivery Methods

The Opportunity for Non-Invasive Surfactant Delivery

While surfactant replacement therapy has transformed the treatment of RDS, significant challenges remain in how therapy is delivered to preterm infants.1,3

Advanced treatment approaches are being developed that could deliver surfactant therapy while minimizing invasiveness and reducing the need for mechanical ventilation.3,5,6

A non-invasive method of surfactant delivery has the potential to simplify treatment, expand access to care, and help more premature infants benefit from surfactant replacement therapy.4-6
