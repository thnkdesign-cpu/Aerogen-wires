The Challenge

Why is Aerosol Drug Delivery So Challenging?

Delivering Aerosol Medicines Is More Complex Than It Appears

Inhaled therapies can deliver medication directly to the lungs, which may decrease systemic exposure and can increase effectiveness when localized effect drives efficacy. However, reliable and reproducible lung dosing can be challenging, particularly in critically ill patients and very small infants.7,8,12

Drug delivery efficiency can be affected by numerous variables, including:6-9

  • Patient size
  • Respiratory support modality and patient interface
  • Respiratory support settings
  • Nebulizer technology
  • Patient breathing patterns
  • Circuit configuration
  • Humidification systems
  • Aerosol generator placement

As a result, the amount of medication that reaches the lungs can vary from patient to patient and across respiratory-support configurations.6-8

Lung Delivery in the Smallest Patients

Small airways, low tidal volumes, rapid respiratory rates, and preferential nasal breathing make aerosol delivery especially challenging in preterm infants. Published studies report very low lung deposition for some less-optimized neonatal aerosol configurations (1 - 10%), while optimized mesh, breath-actuated, and nasal-interface approaches have been developed to increase lung delivery (30 - 50%) in preclinical, bench, and early clinical studies.3,5-7,9,10

Aerosol Loss During Continuous Nebulization

With continuous nebulization, aerosol generated when an infant is not inhaling can be lost to the ventilator circuit or exhalation pathway, limiting the inhaled dose to as little as 1 - 10% of the total aerosol produced. Breath-synchronized aerosol generation is designed to deliver aerosol during inspiration, helping reduce expiratory drug loss and improve delivery efficiency. This approach results in up to 5-fold more emitted drug being inhaled compared with historical continuous nebulizer approaches.6,7,10

How Is the Aerogen Pharma Aerosol Drug Delivery Platform Different?

The Difference Is in the Details

Continuous nebulization can lose aerosol during exhalation and within respiratory circuits, and larger particles are more likely to deposit in the upper airway. In infant and preterm-infant models, smaller particles less than approximately 3 microns have been associated with lower nasal deposition and greater lung delivery than larger particles.6,7,9

Peer-reviewed descriptions of AeroFact and PDAP-based systems report fine aerosols in the approximately 2-3 micron or less than 3 micron range, a particle-size profile intended to support distal pulmonary delivery.5,10,11

Respiratory support systems such as nasal CPAP rely on humidified gas flow and patient interfaces that can dilute aerosol or increase circuit and interface losses. Placing aerosol generation directly at the nasal prongs is one strategy studied to reduce these losses and improve delivery efficiency.5-9

For established therapies where dosing is adjusted based on clinical response, some variability may be managed clinically. For novel drug-device combination products, biologics, and precision therapies, consistent pulmonary delivery is especially important.8,12,13

Aerogen Pharma's technology platform is designed to increase delivery efficiency to the lung, reduce variability, and support confidence in pulmonary drug delivery in critical care environments.5,7,8,10

Platform Design

How Does the Aerogen Pharma Aerosol Drug Delivery Platform Work?

Designed for Precision. Engineered for Performance.

Aerogen Pharma's investigational delivery system combines three integrated components:

Proprietary Vibrating Mesh Nebulizer

A precision-engineered single-patient-use vibrating mesh nebulizer with a proprietary architecture designed to generate highly respirable aerosol particles for pulmonary delivery to infants.5,7,10,11

Intelligent Controller

A reusable controller intended to automate aerosol delivery, monitor dosing, and synchronize drug administration with the patient's breathing pattern.5,10,11

Advanced Patient Interfaces

Custom-designed interfaces position aerosol generation close to the patient's airway, a strategy intended to reduce medication loss and improve delivery efficiency.5-7,9

Together, these components create an integrated platform designed to support reproducible aerosol delivery across patient populations and respiratory-support environments.5,8,10

Core Technology

What is PDAP™ Technology?

The Engine Behind the Platform

At the core of Aerogen Pharma's technology is its proprietary Photo-Defined Aperture Plate (PDAP™) technology.

PDAP is a next-generation vibrating mesh platform intended to provide greater control over aerosol generation and delivery performance.10,11

The PDAP consists of a precision-engineered dual-layer mesh architecture containing thousands of microscopic apertures that, when vibrated at 128,000 cycles per second, transform viscous, liquid formulations into highly respirable aerosol particles (2 - 3 micron).10,11

This design enables control of aerosol characteristics, including droplet size and output rate, to support delivery of challenging inhaled therapies.7,10,11

Potential Advantages of PDAP Technology

  • Generation of highly respirable aerosol particles optimized for lung delivery5,7,10,11
  • Potential to aerosolize viscous solutions and suspensions7,12,13
  • Rapid on-off aerosol generation during each breath, a feature particularly relevant for patients with fast respiratory rates such as infants5,10
  • Potential to improve delivery efficiency for complex and high-cost formulations7,8,10,12,13
  • Potential compatibility with biologics, proteins, peptides, nucleic acids, and other advanced therapeutics12,13
  • Designed to support consistent and reproducible dosing8,10
Future Applications

How Does the Platform Support Future Inhaled Therapies?

Expanding the Possibilities of Inhaled Drug Delivery

Aerosol delivery to preterm and newborn infants remains technically challenging. Many promising therapies were not originally developed for inhaled administration and can be difficult to aerosolize effectively, particularly when formulations have complex physicochemical properties.3,7,12,13

Aerogen Pharma's platform was designed to support delivery of challenging formulations that may have characteristics such as:7,12,13

  • High viscosity12,13
  • Low surface tension12,13
  • Large molecular size12,13
  • Complex biologic structures12,13

The AeroFact Delivery System capabilities are being studied through the APC-0101 Development Program, where PDAP technology is being evaluated as part of an investigational non-invasive aerosolized surfactant drug-device combination approach for preterm infants with respiratory distress syndrome.4,5

Key Platform Features

Precision Aerosol Generation

Produces highly respirable aerosol particles in the approximately 1-3 micron range, a size range commonly targeted for efficient pulmonary delivery.5-7,10,11

Breath-Synchronized Delivery

The aerosol particle generation is coordinated with the first milliseconds of patient inspiration to maximize lung deposition and minimize waste during exhalation.5,10,11

Airway-Level Aerosol Generation

Places aerosol production close to the patient interface to reduce losses within the respiratory circuit.5-7,9

Automated Dosing Control

Provides clinicians with standardized and reproducible aerosol administration.8,10

Modular Platform Architecture

Designed for use across multiple drug candidates, disease states, and patient populations.8,12,13

Compatibility Across Care Settings

Engineered for use across respiratory support modalities, including non-invasive respiratory support and mechanical ventilation.5,8,11
